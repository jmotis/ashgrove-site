<p class="lede">For fifty summers, Ashgrove has been a place where Girl Scouts pitch tents, sing loud, learn knots, and make the kind of friends you keep for life.</p>

Founded in 1976 by a handful of leaders who wanted a camp run by Scouts, for Scouts, Ashgrove has grown into a ten-day program serving roughly 250 campers each summer. Every year carries a theme that ties together songs, badges, costumes, and activities — past themes include "Around the World in 10 Days," "Storybook Summer," and 2026's "High Seas, Deep Seas."

## How it works

Campers are placed in *units* by grade level. Each unit has its own name (often a play on the year's theme), unit leaders, and a daily rhythm: morning flag, unit time, all-camp lunch, afternoon activities, and an end-of-day campfire. Units design their own costumes and unit cheers to perform at all-camp gatherings.

## Run by volunteers

Every staff member at Ashgrove — from the Camp Director to the unit leaders to the lifeguards — is a volunteer. Many are former campers or Girl Scout alumni. We're recognized by Girl Scouts Nation's Capital and are open to any registered Girl Scout in our service area.

### Camp Aides & AITs

High school Scouts can serve as **Camp Aides**, helping unit leaders run programming. 8th-grade Scouts can apply to be **Aides in Training (AITs)** — a structured leadership program that prepares them to step into Camp Aide roles in subsequent summers.

## Location

We meet at **Camp Crowell**, a Girl Scouts Nation's Capital property tucked between Difficult Run and the Cross County Trail in Oakton, VA. Camp grounds include open meadows, wooded trails, a dining hall, multiple unit shelters, and a swimming pool used during all-camp swim time.
